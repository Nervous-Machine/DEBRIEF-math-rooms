# Rooms and hinges: a small Lean proof built from a fixed prior under three error-signal regimes

The shape of a recent machine-built proof of the Riemann hypothesis suggested a search sized to a fast exact error signal, with retries concentrated at the joins. That reading came from one archive with no attempt log. Here the prior is fixed, the plan is drawn once by one model, the cost of each step is predicted and sealed, and a second model searches the plan under full Lean feedback, under accept or reject only, and with no checker until the end. The first target is the Dirac–Erdős bound, Erdős Problem 766, whose Lean statement and only Lean proof both appeared after the searcher model's training cutoff. Result: all three regimes produced the statement as given, the no-checker regime at first try, so the hypothesis as filed is refuted for this build. The signal changed hedging and cost, not success.
